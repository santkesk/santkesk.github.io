Personal project · 2026

Portfolio Website

Designing a better way to share my work

At a Glance

  • Problem I needed one place to present my UX projects, skills and CV to recruiters and collaborators.
  • What I did Planned the site and directed its design step by step with Claude, then reviewed and edited the result.
  • Result A responsive, keyboard-friendly portfolio with project pages, a dark mode and a contact form.

Project Description

This portfolio is a living record of my projects and professional development. I initially worked with Claude to translate my CV and project reports into a static website, directing the content and design decisions myself.

Timeline

2026

Type

Web Development

Project Type

Problem Statement

A CV lists qualifications, but it rarely communicates how a researcher arrives at a finding or how a designer makes a decision. I wanted to make these processes clear to a recruiter who might have only a few minutes to explore my work.

A PDF CV alone cannot show the process behind a project or my skills in context. A UX professional’s own website is also judged on its usability, so the site had to work well for everyone, including keyboard and screen reader users.

My Contribution

I led the work and Claude wrote the code. I gave Claude my CV and project reports as source material and worked in small steps, asking for one change at a time, such as filling in the project pages, redesigning the contact and about pages, or fixing accessibility issues.

I reviewed every version in the browser and reported problems, for example missing spacing and unstyled pages. I made my own edits to the files, such as the footers and contact details, and decided on the structure, wording and which projects and skills to include. I also asked Claude for improvement suggestions from a recruiter’s and a UX professional’s point of view and chose which ones to apply.

Solution & Recommendations

The site uses one consistent visual style with simple navigation across four pages: home, about, projects and contact. Each project page starts with an “At a Glance” summary of the problem, my work and the result, followed by the same sections in the same order, so the pages are easy to skim and compare. Accessibility and usability were built in:

  • Text colours selected with WCAG contrast requirements in mind (formal conformance not established).
  • A skip-to-content link, visible keyboard focus and a highlighted current page in the navigation
  • Animations that respect the reduced-motion setting and never hide content when JavaScript is off
  • A responsive layout that works from phones to desktops
  • A dark mode that can be switched on from the navigation bar and stays on across all pages
  • Descriptive alt text, page descriptions and link text that says when a link opens in a new tab

The site is made with plain HTML, CSS and a small amount of JavaScript, without frameworks. The contact form is handled by Formspree.

Testing & Validation

I checked each version in the browser and reported layout and styling problems, which were fixed in the next round. Contrast and keyboard support were reviewed against WCAG guidelines.

Outcomes & Learnings

The site brings my work together in a consistent, navigable format. AI-assisted development taught me the value of specifying requirements, reviewing implementation and iterating carefully. The current redesign continues that process, with a stronger research-led identity and shared design system.

LibreOffice

At a Glance

  • Problem LibreOffice’s Options dialog overwhelms novice users with too many options, many of them hidden or irrelevant.

  • Result A Figma prototype with a simple/advanced toggle, search bar and clearer categories. The client saw clear benefits and recommended more research.

Project Description

LibreOffice’s Options dialog holds so many settings that it overwhelms novice users, while some advanced options are hidden and developer-only ones are visible. My team redesigned a simplified version of the dialog with a simple/advanced toggle, a search bar and clearer categories, and evaluated a Figma prototype with users.

Problem Statement

The client asked for a simplified version of the Options dialog that is approachable for novices, while keeping the software accessible, which was extremely important to them. The interface is also dated because there is no design lead, which makes choosing which options to remove difficult.

My Contribution

I evaluated UI components against our efficiency, learnability, flexibility and accessibility goals, planned and conducted user interviews, created the UEQ questionnaire and analysed its results.

Research & Evaluation

We analysed bug tickets from LibreOffice forums and interviewed three users, two who had never used LibreOffice and one who had used it often in the past. In 15–20 minute Zoom sessions they completed tasks such as changing the interface language and default font, then answered questions about the menus.

An affinity diagram showed three main needs: relevant options should be visible, easy to find and intuitive. We created novice, advanced and expert personas and focused mainly on novices.

Solution & Recommendations

Our quality goals were efficiency, learnability, flexibility and accessibility. The redesign has three key features:

  • A toggle between a simple and an advanced view, so users choose the level of detail.
  • A search bar that finds any option, including advanced ones while in the simple view.
  • A reorganised category structure based on the Document Foundation’s design whiteboard, keeping the familiar tree view that users liked.

Testing & Validation

We built an interactive Figma prototype and evaluated it with users through a questionnaire based on the User Experience Questionnaire (UEQ), which measured how well it met our design goals. Feedback from our presentation led us to rename “All options” to “Advanced”. We checked accessibility ourselves before the user evaluation.

Outcomes & Learnings

The client felt our solution offers benefits but is not the final answer. Time did not allow a second round of testing, and the results suggested there is still room to improve. Open issues include simplifying the program-specific menus for Writer, Calc and Impress, and further research with a fuller prototype to confirm the simplified hierarchy covers everyday use.

Solita

Exploring a privacy-conscious Finnish AI assistant

At a Glance

  • Problem Solita needed an on-site AI assistant that answers Kela’s student benefit FAQs in Finnish without external language model APIs.
  • What I did Built the front-end and landing page, tested the system manually and documented the project.
  • Result A working minimum viable product with speech-to-text and text-to-speech, delivered as a Docker application and demonstrated to Solita and Kela.

Project Description

FiLOs (Finnish LLM OnSite) is a chat assistant that answers frequently asked questions about Kela’s student benefits. It runs entirely on local, open-source language models, understands spoken or typed Finnish, and can read its answers aloud. A team of seven students built it for Solita as a software engineering project.

Problem Statement

Solita wanted to explore an AI assistant that could answer student-benefit questions without sending data to external language model APIs. Those APIs are expensive, especially for Finnish, and raise information security concerns when the data is confidential.

The assistant therefore had to run fully on-site, support both spoken and written Finnish, and be easy to deploy inside a Docker container.

My Contribution

I worked on the front-end of the application, created the landing page with HTML and CSS, and documented the project. I also did manual system testing and recorded the interface issues I found so they could be fixed. We worked in two-week sprints with weekly meetings with the customer.

Research & Evaluation

This was a research-heavy project: a lot of time went into studying open-source language models, how they can be fine-tuned and which ones suit on-site use. No Finnish dataset could be obtained, so the team built its own training data manually from the FAQ on Kela’s website.

Solution & Recommendations

The application combines three open-source models: a Finnish speech-to-text model, a TurkuNLP model fine-tuned for answer generation, and Mozilla TTS for reading answers aloud. A React front-end and a FastAPI back-end are deployed together with Docker Compose.

Testing & Validation

The team tested at unit, integration, system and acceptance level, with the customer and end-users involved in acceptance and beta testing. Testing found two major bugs: audio playback could interfere when several messages were played at once (medium severity), and the assistant struggled with heavily misspelled questions or special characters (high severity). Both were still open at delivery.

Outcomes & Learnings

We delivered a working minimum viable product that answers simple Kela FAQ questions with speech-to-text and text-to-speech, and it was demonstrated to Solita and Kela. Ideas for further development include a larger FAQ dataset, handling longer speech input, and adding a retrieval-augmented question answering model.

A key lesson was to scope realistically: we dropped our early plan to train our own GPT model once it proved too ambitious for our time and resources.

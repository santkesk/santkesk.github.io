Project Description

Polar Compass is a concept for an AI health companion for Polar Electro: a family of wearables, starting from a single smart ring, and an augmented reality app where a personalised AI coach guides exercise, diet and wellbeing. My team took the concept from user research to an interactive prototype as part of a human-centered product development course.

Problem Statement

Polar’s brief asked how artificial intelligence could be used in its services to guide users, and whether AI could be connected to exercise, health and wellbeing services to give individual guidance. We designed for a market entry around 2030–2035.

Our main customer segment became newcomers who want to be healthier but do not know where to start.

My Contribution

I did desk research and terminology, conducted interviews and data analysis, wrote user stories, and updated the MVP story map. I also wrote parts of the project diary on interaction modalities, technology, the development plan, and handled the rewards for interview participants.

Research & Evaluation

We started with desk research on wearables and AI in health and fitness, then conducted four structured interviews. I conducted two of them and did the thematic analysis. Four themes emerged: the importance of exercise, the challenges of creating and following a program, the commonness of wearable technology, and the increasing but mixed-trust role of AI.

The findings shaped three personas, point-of-view statements and “How might we…?” questions, which guided ideation.

Solution & Recommendations

Polar Compass is an AI companion that learns from wearable sensor data and user preferences, with an onboarding for beginners, data views, and a social side with groups and challenges. After the concept greenlight meeting, the team dropped the original mobile app and moved to a more futuristic AR and wearable solution.

Testing & Validation

We first validated the MVP with four Wizard of Oz sessions and a survey. Personalising the AI increased users’ sense of familiarity, and we learned that the reliability of the AI’s advice needed to be emphasised.

For the final prototype, 12 participants completed a remote test and questionnaire, analysed with an affinity diagram. 11 of 12 understood the product idea and 9 of 12 would use an AI coach. Recurring themes were simple, beginner-friendly, versatile, beneficial AI and statistical.

Outcomes & Learnings

Polar greenlit the concept and we delivered a final presentation, a concept video and an interactive Figma prototype. Next steps would be more user research, the social features as the first new priority, and a bigger AR prototype tested with a Wizard of Oz AI.

Project Description

This project aimed to find usability issues on the TOAS website and improve the overall user experience. Each of the group members conducted a heuristic evaluation of the website, identifying key areas for improvement. Based on these findings, we compiled a comprehensive report detailing the usability issues. Then, we performed user testing with interviews to validate our findings and gather additional insights. Finally, we proposed actionable recommendations to enhance the website’s usability and accessibility.

Problem Statement

TOAS, a student housing service provider in Tampere, needed to enhance the usability of their website to better serve their users, who are mostly students under 25 searching for and applying to apartments.

My Contribution

Besides my heuristic evaluation, I planned the usability test and its test tasks, and conducted the user tests in the roles of moderator, observer and technical support. I also interviewed the participants, documented the sessions and reported the findings.

Research & Evaluation

In our heuristic evaluations, we identified in total 30 usability issues based on Jakob Nielsen’s 10 usability heuristics. These were categorized by the functionality they affected and severity level.

In my own evaluation I found six issues, for example that the apartment size fields in the application look editable but are not, that rents are not shown in the application form, and that an unclear error appears when switching application type.

Solution & Recommendations

Based on the evaluations and user tests, we proposed actionable recommendations to improve the website’s usability and accessibility. They included allowing one application to cover several apartment types, sorting search results by rent, marking favourites in the application form, and improving the site search.

Testing & Validation

We conducted five user tests, the first one being a pilot test, with students from Tampere University. Each group member worked either as the moderator (leading the interview), observer (taking notes), or technical support. Participants were asked to complete 11 specific tasks on the TOAS website while thinking aloud. After each test, we interviewed the participants and gathered qualitative feedback on their experiences, frustrations, and suggestions for improvement.

The tasks covered information search, filtering apartments and applying for housing. The application tasks caused the most problems: every participant needed a separate application for each apartment type and could not see their favourites in the form. Search results also could not be sorted by rent. The SUS questionnaire averaged 59, below the benchmark of 68.

Outcomes & Learnings

We compiled a report on the 30 usability issues we found, and user testing validated the key ones. The findings were presented to TOAS with recommendations for changes. TOAS appreciated the insights, and some of our recommendations were implemented afterwards.
